## 1. Mathematical rigour and proof consistency

### 1.1 Assumption architecture (A1–A6, S1–S2, O1–O5)

The three-tier structure (common / ASGS-specific / OSGS-specific) is well conceived, and the "where each hypothesis is used" bookkeeping (`rem:hypotheses`, and the OSGS commentary's hypothesis map) is exemplary. The following observations concern completeness, necessity, and redundancy.

- **F1 — (S1) carries a vestigial parameter.** The condition $c_1>2\xi C_{\mathrm{inv}}^2$ "for some $\xi>2$" is, as the appendix itself notes, *equivalent* to $c_1>4\overline C_{\mathrm{inv}}^2$; the $\xi$ plays no further role (the choice $\eta=4$ makes both coefficients $1-4\overline C_{\mathrm{inv}}^2/c_1$). Either state (S1) as $c_1\ge 4(1+\delta)\overline C_{\mathrm{inv}}^2$ with an explicit margin $\delta>0$ on which $C_{\mathrm{coer}}$ depends, or simply $c_1>4\overline C_{\mathrm{inv}}^2$. *(Should-fix; cosmetic but it is the kind of thing a SINUM referee notices.)*
- **F2 — (S2) is derivable from the standing assumptions, asymptotically.** The jump condition's content (facewise comparability of $\varphi_1=\alpha_K\tau_{1,\mathrm{NS}}^{-1}$, with $\sigma$ cancelling in the jump of $\tau_1^{-1}$) is exactly `oa:lem:patch` restricted to face-adjacent elements, derived there from (A1)+(A3)+(A4) over the viscous floor $\nu>0$. However, the derived constants degrade with $\nu$ and the modulus of continuity of $\boldsymbol a$ (the same data-dependence as $\psi(h)$), whereas keeping (S2) as a *hypothesis with fixed constants* is what preserves the ASGS row "all $h$ (unconditional)" in Table `tab:ASGSvsOSGS`. **Recommendation:** keep (S2) but add a two-line remark stating the implication and its caveat; this converts an apparent asymmetry between the hypothesis sets into a documented design choice, and pre-empts the referee question "why does one method need a jump condition the other's lemmas prove?" The commented appendix contains precisely this analysis — promote it into the plain text.
- **F3 — The nodal-Lagrange structure is a hidden hypothesis of (O3)/the smoothing operator.** (A5) demands only "continuous" spaces of given degree; the smoothing product `oa:eq:SmulDef` presumes a nodal basis with nodal values, and admissibility of the witness ($\bw_h\in V_{h0}$ because "the smoothed product preserves nodal zeros") presumes that zero boundary *nodal values* imply zero *trace* — true for Lagrange elements, not for arbitrary continuous bases. The commentary flags this; the plain text does not. **Fix:** add "nodal Lagrange" to (A5) or to (O3).
- **F4 — (A5)'s constraint $k_p\le k_u+1$ is exercised only by the OSGS consistency estimate** (best approximation with $z=\nabla p$, $r=k_p$ in the degree-$k_u$ velocity projection space). It is harmless as a common assumption, but a one-clause annotation ("used only in `oa:lem:bestapprox`") would sharpen the architecture. Similarly "constants belong to the pressure space" is exercised by both mean-shift constructions — fine as common.
- **F5 — (O1)'s compressibility clause duplicates (A2).** $\varepsilon\tau_{2,K}\le C_2$ is implied by `eq:UpperBoundOnEpsilon` via (P: eps); the text says "consistently with (A2)", which is accurate, but stating it as an *independent* clause of (O1) invites the question of whether the OSGS route needs less than the ASGS $\varepsilon$-bound. It does (only the weaker product bound is used in the stability proof), and saying so explicitly would be a small but genuine sharpening of the comparison.
- **F6 — (O2) is assumed, never verified for the pairs computed with.** The Codina–Blasco projection-stability condition is correctly identified as the one genuinely method-specific hypothesis, and the mass-slot "$\beta_0=1$" observation is elegant. But the manuscript is silent on the *status* of (O2) for equal-order continuous Lagrange pairs on the meshes used: is it proven anywhere (and under what mesh conditions), numerically observed, or open? A referee will ask. Cite what [codina2008analysis] and successors establish, and state honestly which case the experiments occupy. *(Should-fix; scientific completeness.)*
- **F7 — Well-posedness of the continuous linearized problem is assumed only implicitly** (through (A6): a solution with the stated regularity exists). For $\varepsilon=0$ this rests on the continuous inf–sup for the weighted divergence $b(\boldsymbol v,q)=(q,\nabla\!\cdot(\alpha\boldsymbol v))$, which *does* hold under (A3) — given $q\in L^2_0$, take $\boldsymbol w$ with $\nabla\!\cdot\boldsymbol w=q$ and set $\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol w/\alpha\in H^1_0$ using $\alpha\in W^{1,\infty}$, $\alpha\ge\alpha_0$. A three-line remark to this effect would close the only genuinely tacit link in the chain "continuous problem → discrete analysis". *(Should-fix.)*
- **F8 — Constant $\sigma$ is load-bearing in two different ways** and this could be surfaced once, centrally: for OSGS it is *essential* (the annihilation lemma fails for variable $\sigma$, as the commentary notes); for ASGS it is a simplification (elementwise-constant $\sigma$ would plausibly suffice with $\widetilde\sigma_\alpha$ elementwise, but this is not claimed). A sentence in §5.1 distinguishing "essential to the mechanism" from "assumed for simplicity" per assumption would strengthen the necessity discussion the referee expects.

### 1.2 The ASGS chain (coercivity route)

**Dependency map (verified):** (A1)–(A4) + `eq:UpperBoundOnEpsilon` + (S1) → `lem:coercivity` (all $h$); (A1)–(A4) + (S2, if $\sigma>0$) with only $c_1,c_2>0$ → `lem:continuity` / `lem:continterp`; coercivity + exact consistency `eq:consistency` + `lem:continterp` + (A6) → `thm:convergence` in $\Psi_{\mathrm S}(h)$. The chain is complete and the appendix proof is correct at every step checked (V4–V6). Specific remarks:

- **F9 — `lemma:Continuity` (discrete–discrete) is stated in the main text but plays no role in the convergence chain**; only the interpolation-error variant `lem:continterp` does, as the text itself notes. Either state its independent purpose (it is the porous counterpart of [codina2001] Lemma 2 and documents the sharpened $\alpha_K$-weighted right-hand side) or demote it to the appendix and reference it from the main text. As it stands, a careful reader wonders why a lemma is stated whose modified version is what is used.
- **F10 — `th:Convergence`'s framing of the exact solution is convoluted.** "Let $U$ be the exact solution of the linearized problem corresponding to `eq:DiscretizedNonlinearStabilizedEquation`, where $\boldsymbol u^{m-1}$ is replaced by a given $\boldsymbol a$…" — the exact solution solves the *continuous* linearized problem, not a discretized equation. Restate: "Let $U$ solve the continuous linearized problem of §5.1 (advection $\boldsymbol a$, $\nabla\!\cdot(\alpha\boldsymbol a)=0$, constant $\sigma$, homogeneous Dirichlet data)…".
- **F11 — For $k_u=1$ the "viscous residual" degenerates instructively and this is never said.** For $\mathbb P_1$ velocities, $\Pi^{\mathrm{DS}}\nabla\boldsymbol u_h$ is elementwise constant, so $\nabla\!\cdot(\alpha\Pi^{\mathrm{DS}}\nabla\boldsymbol u_h)=(\Pi^{\mathrm{DS}}\nabla\boldsymbol u_h)\nabla\alpha$: the enlarged constant $\overline C_{\mathrm{inv}}=\sqrt{d\delta_\alpha}\,C_{\mathrm{inv}}+C_{\nabla\alpha}$ effectively reduces to $C_{\nabla\alpha}$, and the coercivity constraint on $c_1$ is porosity-resolution-driven rather than geometry-driven. One remark to this effect would (a) explain why $c_1=4$ suffices comfortably for $\mathbb P_1$ even in 3D while $\mathbb P_2$ tetrahedra need $16k^4$, and (b) tie §5 to the §7.2 footnote quantitatively. *(Nice-to-have; genuinely clarifying.)*

### 1.3 The OSGS chain (inf–sup route)

**Dependency map (verified):** (A1)–(A5) + (O1)–(O3) → `oa:th:stability` (conditional: $h\le h_0$, sufficient non-sharp $\gamma_0$); + (A6) + (O4)–(O5) → consistency `oa:lem:consistency` and interpolation continuity `oa:lem:interpolation`, both landing directly in the $\ell^2$ functional $\Psi_{\mathrm O}$; two-line endgame → `oa:th:convergence`; corollaries `oa:cor:asgsnorm`, `oa:cor:Ltwo`. Every step checked (V7–V10) is correct, and the Step-4 audit was confirmed uniformly in $M_2$ (V9). The scoping discipline — "conditional stability theorem, not a calibration"; the untracked-constants caveat in `oa:rem:numerics`; the one-sidedness caveats in `oa:rem:mechanism` and `oa:rem:open`(iii) — is exactly right and should not be weakened under referee pressure. Remaining points:

- **F12 — The norm-level "forcing" of the Damköhler factor is proved only in the commentary layer, and the plain text underclaims relative to it.** The commented appendix observes (at `oa:lem:interpsize`) that the reactive component of the *interpolation error alone*, measured in $\tnorm\cdot_{\mathrm O}$, is already $\mathrm{Da}_{h,K}^{1/2}$ (up to convective factors, since $\sigma^{1/2}=\mathrm{Da}_{h,K}^{1/2}(\alpha_K/h_K)\tau_2^{1/2}/(1+(c_2/c_1)\mathrm{Re}_{h,K})^{1/2}$ *exactly*) times the ASGS-type functional; since no estimate in this norm can beat best approximation, the factor is intrinsic to the *norm*, not to the proof strategy. The plain `oa:rem:mechanism` says only "not established as sharp or necessary". These are compatible (sharpness of the norm-vs-functional relation ≠ sharpness of the discrete-error estimate), but the norm-level forcing is a genuine, cheap theorem-let that pre-empts the referee suggestion "sharpen the estimates to remove the factor". **Promote it into the plain appendix as a short remark, keeping the distinction between the two notions of sharpness explicit.**
- **F13 — Main-text theorem statements over-assume slightly.** `th:StabilityOSGS` says "Under the standing assumptions of §5.1", which sweeps in (A6), (O4), (O5) — none needed for stability (the appendix statement correctly lists (A1)–(A5), (O1)–(O3)). Align the main-text statement with the appendix's hypothesis list; the comparison table's "extra hypotheses" row already gets this right.
- **F14 — `eq:BosgsMain` is introduced as "the linearized, constant-coefficient problem analyzed here"** — but $\alpha(\boldsymbol x)$ is emphatically not constant. "Constant-coefficient" should read "constant $\sigma$ and $\varepsilon$, prescribed $\boldsymbol a$" (the porosity remains variable; that is the point of the paper).
- **F15 — The analyzed-vs-implemented ledger (`oa:rem:analyzed`) is excellent and should be advertised from the main text.** Items (i) weighted vs plain projection, (ii) Method-I truncation, (iii) converged vs lagged projection, (iv) unconstrained projection space, (v) elementwise vs pointwise $\tau$ constitute exactly the "variational crimes" inventory a referee will construct; the main text's scope paragraph before §7 covers (ii)–(v) but not (i). Add one clause referencing (i) there.

### 1.4 Porosity dependence ($\alpha_0$, $\delta_\alpha$, $C_{\nabla\alpha}$)

Verified sharp and consistently threaded: the enlarged inverse constant $\overline C_{\mathrm{inv}}=\sqrt{d\delta_\alpha}\,C_{\mathrm{inv}}+C_{\nabla\alpha}$ with the documented abuse of notation (`rem:winvconst`); the multiplicative patch equivalence with constant $\delta_\alpha^2$ *independent of the contrast* $\alpha_\infty/\alpha_0$ (a genuinely nice feature, worth one advertising clause); the $M_2=1+C_{\nabla\alpha}/C_{\mathrm{inv}}$ surcharge in $\gamma_0$; the $\alpha_K^2$ weight in `eq:UpperBoundOnEpsilon` matching the $\alpha_K$-weighted continuity (the observation in `rem:sharper` is a highlight); and the elementwise $\alpha_K^2$ weighting of $\Psi_{\mathrm S}$/$\Psi_{\mathrm O}$ with the honest worst-case conversion to $\alpha_0^{-1/2}$ (single documented exception $1/\alpha_0$). One residual point:

- **F16 — The two $C_{\mathrm{inv}}$'s in (S1) vs (O1) are different objects (enlarged vs plain), and this is disclosed only in a footnote to a remark.** The disclosure is correct and honest; but since `eq:OSGSDesignCondition` and `eq:conditions_on_num_param` are compared side by side as "the same left-hand quantity $2k^2$", the different right-hand meanings deserve main-text prose, not a footnote — otherwise the reader may erroneously conclude the OSGS condition is uniformly the stronger one for a *common* constant.

## 2. Physical scaling and asymptotics

### 2.1 The Fourier design of $\tau_1$, $\tau_2$

The heuristic is presented with unusual care (the `≈`-symbol footnote; the honest factor-5 amendment in `eq:FTOfDifferentialOperator` with the correct "reduces to it when one operator dominates" reading; the self-referential $\tau_{b,1}^{-1}$ cancellation openly discussed; the $(2-2/d)$ factor tracked, with $d=2$ exact). Appendix B (V1–V3) is correct throughout. Remaining points, all exposition-level:

- **F17 — The $\tau_b$ "ratio $=\lambda$" condition is presented as a free choice, but it is actually forced by matching *both* generalized eigenvalues.** The two nonzero $\Lambda^{-1}$-generalized eigenvalues of $\widehat{\mathcal L}_b^\dagger\Lambda\widehat{\mathcal L}_b$ coincide at $\lambda(\alpha|k_0|/h)^2$ (verified, V2); imposing the diagonal ansatz to match them both yields exactly `eq:ftTauB`, whereas matching the spectral radius alone leaves the ratio genuinely underdetermined. One sentence connecting the two design conditions to the stated criterion (radius + kernel, refined to full eigenvalue matching for this block) would make §B.4 self-contained.
- **F18 — The metric $\Lambda$ is element- and iterate-dependent once $\lambda$ is fixed by `eq:ftLambda`, and this is never acknowledged.** Since $\lambda=h^2/(|k_0|^2\tau_{1,\mathrm{NS}}^2)$ depends on $h_K$, $\nu$, $|\boldsymbol w|$, the "dimensional bookkeeping metric" varies across the mesh and the nonlinear iteration. Harmless for a heuristic, but a parenthetical would forestall the pedantic objection that the norm in `eq:BoundProjectionOfLBySubscales` changes under the integral sign's feet.
- **F19 — The assignment of the $\nabla\alpha$ block to the *momentum* eigenvalue deserves one sentence of justification.** $\mathbf S_{\nabla\alpha}=\boldsymbol e_n(\nabla\alpha)^\top$ acts on the *mass* row, yet its spectral contribution $\sqrt\lambda\,|\nabla\alpha|$ is booked into $\tau_1^{-1}$ (correctly, per the diagonal-ansatz operator norm and kernel condition — verified), producing $C_\alpha$. Since a reader's first instinct is "mass-equation term → $\tau_2$", spell out that the diagonal approximation's operator norm is realised on the velocity components, and that the kernel condition ($\Pi$-pressure column in both kernels) fixes $\tau_{\nabla\alpha,2}^{-1}=0$.

### 2.2 Asymptotic regimes (verified)

All three regimes were re-derived (V12), including the amended prefactors, the two-regime disclaimer (fixed-data $h\to0$ vs pre-asymptotic parameter regimes) — which is a model of honest framing — and the $\mathrm{Da}\propto\mathrm{Da}_h L^2/h^2$ mitigation-factor mechanics. The screening-length reading $\mathrm{Da}_{h,K}=(h_K/\ell_\sigma)^2$, $\ell_\sigma=(\alpha_K\nu/\sigma)^{1/2}$ (`oa:rem:robustness`) is the best physical sentence in the paper and could profitably appear in the main-text §6 reaction discussion, not only in the appendix. Findings:

- **F20 — `eq:DominantReactionOSGSVelocity` silently drops the $(1+c_1/\mathrm{Da}_h)$ factor of `oa:cor:Ltwo`.** In the $\mathrm{Da}_h\to\infty$ section this is legitimate (the factor is $\le 1+c_1$ uniformly for $\mathrm{Da}_h\ge1$ and $\to1$), and the surrounding prose says so, but the display itself should carry either the factor or a "$\mathrm{Da}_h\gtrsim c_1$" qualifier, since it is the only main-text display of the corollary and will be quoted out of its paragraph.
- **F21 — Abstract phrasing "error estimates that are robust with respect to the Reynolds and Damköhler numbers" overstates what §6 delivers.** The ASGS estimate degrades (slowly, $\sim\mathrm{Re}_h^{1/2}$ in the pressure at strong reaction); the OSGS gradient bound carries $(c_1+\mathrm{Da}_{h,K})^{1/2}$; §6 itself instructs the reader to interpret "robust" against the stated regime. The body's discipline should reach the abstract: e.g. "error estimates whose dependence on the Reynolds and Damköhler numbers is traced explicitly, with $\sigma$-robust $L^2$ velocity control", which is both accurate and arguably a stronger selling point to this referee pool.
- **F22 — The DBF resistance realisation `eq:CocquetMMSReaction` leaves the dimensional bookkeeping to the reader.** As written, $a(\alpha)$, $b(\alpha)$ are dimensionless and $b|\boldsymbol u|$ has velocity units; the claimed $\mathrm{Da}(\alpha_0)=C_a((1-\alpha_0)/\alpha_0)^2+C_b((1-\alpha_0)/\alpha_0)$ (numerically verified: 2.05, 40.05) only follows once one reconstructs the implicit factors — evidently $\sigma=(\alpha_\infty\nu/L^2)\bigl[C_a(\cdot)^2+C_b(\cdot)\,|\boldsymbol u|/U\bigr]$ or equivalent, "$|\boldsymbol u|=U$" meaning the dimensionless modulus equals one. State the dimensional $\sigma$ once, explicitly, with the $\nu$, $U$, $L$, $\alpha_\infty$ factors in place; the current "factor $\nu$ that we keep implicit" under-specifies (it is not only $\nu$ that is implicit).
- **F23 — Two table-level loose ends in §7.3.** (i) The TH pressure slope $3.73$ at $(\mathrm{Re},\alpha_0)=(1,0.1)$ exceeds even the optimal $k_p+1=2$ by a wide margin — structured-mesh superconvergence, presumably pre-asymptotic; a one-clause footnote avoids the appearance of an unremarked anomaly. (ii) The isolation logic of the closing paragraph reads self-contradictorily: the $\mathbb P_2/\mathbb P_1$-ASGS control run *does* isolate the stabilization effect (as ¶"Convection-dominated" says), yet the final sentence states "This comparison does not isolate the effect of the element pair". Both are true of *different* comparisons (headline vs control), but the wording should say so: "the headline equal-order-vs-TH rows alone do not isolate the pair effect; the $\mathbb P_2/\mathbb P_1$-ASGS control row supplies that isolation."

## 3. Structure, exposition, and clarity

The paper's expository architecture is strong: the abstract-operator framing pays off in the VMS derivation; §5.1's common-setting subsection with the three-tier assumptions is the right design; Table `tab:ASGSvsOSGS` is the best single page of the paper; the scope paragraphs bracketing §7 ("one-sided upper bounds… consistency in order of magnitude, not verification") set a standard of epistemic hygiene most published stabilized-FEM papers do not meet. The interpolation-benchmark methodology (nodal-interpolant reference rows, the quadratic excess indicator, the $\sqrt6$ caveat on $L^2$ nodal optimality) is a genuine methodological contribution to how such campaigns should be reported. Against that backdrop, the defects:

**Mechanical defects (must fix):**

- **F24 — Build configuration: `article_v2.tex` inputs the *commented* OSGS appendix with the commentary switched on.** Line 1968 reads `\input{osgs_appendix_commented.tex}` and the file ships with `\pedagogyshowtrue`. As configured, the compiled article typesets every commentary box — including meta-text such as "Boxes marked 'Audit note' flag wording the commentator believes could be tightened" — into the submission PDF. Switch to `\input{osgs_appendix.tex}` (or flip `\pedagogyshowfalse`), and add a build assertion so this cannot regress. The two files' mathematical content was verified identical at the label/environment level (V15).
- **F25 — `\label{eq:TripleNormASGS}` sits *outside* its `equation` environment** (line 990, after `\end{equation}`): the equation environment is a group, so the label binds to the enclosing context, not the equation number. The bug is currently dormant (the label is never `\cref`'d — verified), but the equation is numbered and unreferenceable; move the label inside. While there, note that the norm's defining display and the definitions of $X(U_h)$, $\widetilde\sigma_\alpha$ sit *outside* `lemma:Stability` yet are referenced within its statement — restructure so the lemma is self-contained (define the norm first, then state the lemma), matching the OSGS section's cleaner order.
- **F26 — `eq:OSGSProjection` displays the projection onto the *constrained* space $\mathcal X_{h0}$, while footnote, implementation, and analysis all use the *unconstrained* spaces.** The footnote after `eq:OSGSProblem` and `oa:rem:analyzed`(iv) patch this, but a definition-level display that the paper's own appendix must correct is a defect: introduce the unconstrained product space $\mathcal X_h$ in §3.2 and let the display, `eq:NonlinearResidualProjection`'s "$\forall W_h$", the footnote (demoted to a why-remark), and the analysis all agree.

## 5. Overall assessment

**Mathematics:** sound. Every derivation checked in this audit — spanning the Fourier design, both stability routes, both convergence chains, the corollaries, and the robustness limits — is correct, and the two proof appendices are unusually complete, with hypothesis-usage bookkeeping and analyzed-vs-implemented ledgers that most published stabilized-FEM analyses lack. The comparative ASGS/OSGS architecture (coercivity vs inf–sup; damped vs full reaction norm; exact vs defect consistency; the $(c_1+\mathrm{Da}_{h,K})^{1/2}$ mechanism traced to the annihilation lemma) is the paper's core theoretical contribution and it is executed cleanly.

**Empirics:** the campaign is genuinely stronger than a rate check — the interpolation-benchmark methodology, the excess indicator, and the disciplined one-sided-bound epistemics are model practice — and the two adverse observations (the $\mathbb P_1$ reaction-regime OSGS excess; the 3D OSGS pressure-gradient stagnation) are reported rather than buried, which will earn trust with referees provided the open-question framing stands.

**Blocking issues:** none mathematical. The five P0 items are mechanical (build configuration, one label, one definition-level display, one dimension convention, markup hygiene) and a day's work. The P1 items are where the referee reports will otherwise concentrate: the status of (O2), the tacit continuous well-posedness, the abstract's "robust", and the notation collisions.

**Venue fit:** with the P0/P1 items addressed, the manuscript is a credible SINUM submission (the linearized analyses carry it) and a strong CMAME one (the method + campaign carry it); for SINUM, the supplementary-material relocation of Appendix A (F30) and the compression of the duplicated OSGS collection block would improve fit without touching content.
